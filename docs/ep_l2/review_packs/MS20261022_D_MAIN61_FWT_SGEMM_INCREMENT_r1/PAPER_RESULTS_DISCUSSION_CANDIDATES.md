# English candidates, separate from EN-v22

**Finite-system results.** Completing FWT restores six of the seven preregistered MAIN inputs on the same main61 binary. FWT decreases from 493,189 to 490,402 cycles (1.005683×), giving an accepted-six geometric mean of 0.999054×. The adverse CFD, DWT and Vector results remain included. The original-seven aggregate remains unknown because Btree is on hold. Separately preregistered SGEMM exploration decreases from 343,730 to 343,442 cycles (1.000839×); it does not replace Btree or enter the MAIN denominator. These finite-system observations are separate from the previously reported joint capacity/data-service diagnostic.

**Mechanism evidence.** FWT eliminates 178,569 copy reads and 178,569 copy writes, reduces physical bank service by 3.49%, and reduces lower read bytes by 8.48%. However, lower writeback bytes increase by 16.23%, causing total lower read-plus-writeback traffic to increase by 2.10%. The full design serves 184,660 victim reads, including 12,489 dirty-forward responses, and performs 37,675 promotions. SGEMM has no measured copying or writeback in either condition; its full design serves 35,936 clean-victim reads, yet lower reads decrease by only 4,464 and execution improves by 0.084%. Service-event volumes therefore should not be equated with independently avoided misses or proportional cycle savings.

**Discussion and boundaries.** Both full-design endpoints have zero certified RO admission and zero successful Tag detach; the actual readonly opportunity remains unknown under their empty certificates. More request-wait observations coexist with modestly lower application cycles, so overlapping queue, bank and lookup statistics cannot be summed into an exclusive stall decomposition. The S/F comparison changes the complete finite design and does not isolate W or V causality. No per-kernel timing attribution is made because ordered elapsed phase data are absent from the accepted metadata export. These measurements concern the qualified frozen-trace model; they do not establish numerical host-output equivalence or a hardware performance upper bound.
